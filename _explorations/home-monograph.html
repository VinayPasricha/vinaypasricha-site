An introduction, in five paragraphs

The shape of thirty‑five years.

In 1990 I rented a single room in Delhi and put a name on the door. That door, slowly, became a college. WLC College India. By the time I sold it, more than fifty thousand people had walked through it. None of them were customers. All of them were proof.

The college taught me that execution is the only discipline. Ideas are cheap. Plans are romantic. The thing that compounds is the quiet daily work of making the next small thing arrive.

I carried that lesson into a media business. Then a hiring platform — GoodSpace AI — which has now matched more than half a million people to work. The work was the same. Only the surface changed.

Somewhere along the way I started writing. First privately, in notebooks. Then publicly. Four books now exist. Each one is an attempt to extract a single instrument from the noise of three and a half decades.

I am still building. I am still writing. I remain, stubbornly and by temperament, a contrarian.
