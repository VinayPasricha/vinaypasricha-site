Summary

In this Hindi Short, Vinay Pasricha describes a family whose land is grabbed. The lawyer promises a strong case, then the dates keep coming, and by the time the verdict arrives their hair has turned grey. He says this is the reality for crores of Indian families, stating that more than 5 crore cases are pending, roughly one case for every 25 Indians. Civilisation rests on a simple link: a crime happens and a court delivers a verdict. In India, he argues, that link is breaking. Victims pay the price, and lakhs of people sit in jail for years awaiting trial. As trust in courts erodes, people demand instant action, even the bullet, and where people trust the gun more than the court, the rule of law weakens. Citing 22 judges per million people, he calls for modern courts and for judicial reform as a national priority: "India doesn't need mob justice; it needs courts that work."

Key points

  • He states that more than 5 crore cases are pending, about one for every 25 Indians.
  • Endless delay breaks the basic link between crime and verdict.
  • Loss of trust in courts drives public demand for encounters, which weakens the rule of law.
  • With 22 judges per million people, he calls for modern courts and judicial reform as a national priority.
