Summary

Speaking in Hindi, Vinay Pasricha says up front that this video is not about the BJP, the Congress or any party. Parties, governments and prime ministers change, but the Indian state stays largely the same, and that is the real problem. He cites a recent report that some teenagers, one only 16, found flaws in India's examination systems from their bedrooms. Officers, contractors, consultants and auditors had either missed those flaws or failed to fix them. Running fair, reliable exams is among the most basic duties of a modern state, yet every year brings NEET controversies, paper leaks, technical failures and administrative confusion. Exams, he says, are only the start: citizens wait years for approvals, court cases run for decades and police reform has been discussed for generations. He then asks how citizens can trust the same state on national security, borders, intelligence, the power grid and cyber systems when its visible systems leak and the official response is to hide the problems, relying on what one student called "security through obscurity". A state earns trust on what we cannot see by being competent at what we can. He argues that India lacks neither talent nor resources. The problem is a system that rewards compliance over competence, procedure over outcomes, hierarchy over accountability and power over service. The real tragedy is that people have got used to it. He asks whether anyone has the courage to change the system itself, and urges viewers to share the video.

Key points

  • The problem isn't any one party: governments change, but the Indian state and its institutions stay the same.
  • Teenagers reportedly found exam-system flaws that officials, contractors and auditors missed. NEET controversies and paper leaks recur every year.
  • If visible systems leak and the response is "security through obscurity", citizens have little basis to trust the state on hidden systems like national security.
  • India lacks neither talent nor resources. The system rewards compliance over competence, procedure over outcomes and hierarchy over accountability.
  • The real tragedy is that citizens have got used to failure. The question is who will change the system itself.
