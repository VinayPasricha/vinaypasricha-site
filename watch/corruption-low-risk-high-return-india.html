Corruption Is a Low-Risk, High-Return Business in India

  • India & Systems
  • 11 June 2026
  • 0:56
  • Hindi
  • Short
  • Justice Delayed

Summary

In this Hindi Short, Vinay Pasricha describes assets seized from the home of a joint director in Madhya Pradesh, including gold jewellery, a two-storey gym, plots and agricultural land. Asked where it came from, the officer credited his wife's sewing and knitting. Vinay's question is why the officer was so comfortable giving that answer. His explanation: the officer knows the explanation doesn't need to be good, only that a process must start, and in India the process is the escape route: inquiry, suspension, appeal, date after date. Two years later the file is still open and the officer is on pension. With conviction rates so low, getting caught is an inconvenience, not a punishment. He argues this is not about one officer or party but a system that has made corruption a low-risk, high-return investment.

Key points

  • An officer explained seized assets as income from his wife's sewing and knitting, and was comfortable doing so.
  • In India the process (inquiry, suspension, appeal, adjournments) is the escape route.
  • Low conviction rates make getting caught an inconvenience, not a punishment.
  • The system, not one officer or party, has made corruption low-risk and high-return.
