Summary

Vinay Pasricha explains that faced with complexity, the mind doesn't sit in the discomfort; it crushes the problem into one small, clean idea so it can feel resolved. "Users left because of price." "That hire failed on attitude." It feels like understanding but is premature closure, which he calls the first distortion. Every decision built on it inherits the crack, and the danger is that it never feels shallow; it feels like being decisive.

Key points

  • The mind compresses complexity into one clean idea to feel resolved.
  • Examples: "users left because of price", "that hire failed on attitude".
  • This premature closure is the first distortion, and decisions built on it inherit the crack.
  • It feels like decisiveness, not shallowness.

Your brain lies to you, and it feels exactly like clarity.
