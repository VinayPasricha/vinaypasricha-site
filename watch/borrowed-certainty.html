Summary

Vinay Pasricha argues that smart people don't fail from a lack of intelligence but from borrowed certainty: taking one confident perspective (their own, their investors', their loudest customers') and treating it as the whole of reality. The fix, he says, isn't to think harder but to think from more places. In the SIV Method he runs every real decision through named lenses, angles of inquiry that force the hidden dimensions of a problem to show themselves.

Key points

  • Borrowed certainty means treating one confident perspective as the whole of reality.
  • The perspective can be your own, your investors' or your loudest customers'.
  • The fix is thinking from more places, not thinking harder.
  • Named lenses surface a problem's hidden dimensions.

One lens gives you confidence, many lenses give you reality.
