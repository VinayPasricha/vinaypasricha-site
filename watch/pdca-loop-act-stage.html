Summary

Vinay Pasricha notes that in the 1950s the plan-do-check-act loop helped rebuild Japan's manufacturing base, and that nearly every serious organisation now claims to run it, though almost none do. They plan, they do and sometimes they check, but the loop breaks at "act", the stage where the system is supposed to get stronger. Act, he says, does not mean trying harder next quarter; it means identifying the one thing that most limited output and redesigning the next cycle so that thing is stronger. That is what Execution Doctrine formalises: each cycle the governing constraint is identified and specifically strengthened, and the system carries more, through method rather than effort. He calls this the capacity expansion engine.

Key points

  • PDCA helped rebuild Japan's manufacturing after the 1950s, but most organisations don't run it properly.
  • The loop usually breaks at the Act stage.
  • "Act" means strengthening the one thing that most limited output, not trying harder.
  • Execution Doctrine formalises this as the capacity expansion engine.

Act does not mean try harder next quarter.
