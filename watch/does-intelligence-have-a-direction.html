The Universe Took 14 Billion Years to Ask This Question

Summary

Vinay Pasricha compresses the universe's 14 billion years into a single calendar year, in which every empire, religion and invention fits into the last few seconds of December 31st. Inside those seconds matter learned to think, and now those same seconds are producing minds that are not biological. Yet one question, he says, has never been asked seriously: does intelligence itself have a direction? His answer, developed in The Civilization, is that intelligence moves along eight identifiable directions wherever it emerges: not political or cultural but universal. Against them a technology, society, nation or species can be read, which makes progress a matter of alignment rather than opinion. A civilization can move very fast in the wrong direction.

Key points

  • On a one-year cosmic calendar, all of human history fits in the last seconds of December 31st.
  • Those seconds now produce non-biological minds.
  • He proposes that intelligence moves along eight universal directions.
  • Progress is a matter of alignment, not opinion; speed in the wrong direction is possible.

A civilization can move very fast in the wrong direction.
