Summary

Vinay Pasricha, founder of GoodSpace AI, asks hiring managers a hard question: when did your interview process last tell you the truth? Every senior candidate in the room, he says, is performing. They have read the job description twice, rehearsed their answers and been coached by a friend in talent acquisition. He calls this "professional theater" and argues that most hiring managers are unknowingly running auditions, not interviews. The core issue is that the skills that win an interview (verbal polish, structured storytelling, fast pattern matching) are not the skills that win on the job in month nine. Companies end up hiring great performers and then wonder six months later why the team feels flat. In his book Organizational Frequency, he argues that the next decade of hiring will be won not by the company with the toughest interview but by the one that learns to discover rather than interrogate. He says GoodSpace AI built its workforce intelligence layer on one line: hiring is not convincing people to fit somewhere, it is discovering where they already belong. He closes by urging viewers to stop running auditions and start running discovery, and points them to the book and the resonance runtime on his website.

Key points

  • Senior candidates arrive rehearsed and coached. Interviews have become "professional theater".
  • Most hiring managers are running auditions, not interviews.
  • Interview skills (verbal polish, storytelling, fast pattern matching) differ from the skills that matter on the job in month nine.
  • The next decade of hiring will be won by companies that discover rather than interrogate.
  • GoodSpace AI's principle: hiring means discovering where people already belong.

Stop running auditions. Start running discovery.
