The Hiring Mistake Every Company Makes (And Marriage Got Right 100 Years Ago)

Summary

Vinay Pasricha compares hiring to how marriages have traditionally been arranged "in our part of the world." Before any meeting, families ask whether the two people come from similar worlds: background, family values, ways of living and celebrating, handling money and handling conflict. Over centuries, he says, people understood that two people don't just have to like each other; their frequencies have to match. Families built systems to check this through networks, long conversations and references from people who knew both sides. Those systems were imperfect, but the intent was right: compatibility checked in depth, over time. He then points out the absurdity of modern hiring. Nobody gets married after reading a one-page profile and having a single 45-minute meeting where both sides are on their best behaviour, yet companies hire exactly that way. One resume and one rehearsed interview lead to a commitment of nine hours a day, five days a week, for years: a marriage-sized decision made with a matrimonial-ad-sized process. He acknowledges that a job is not a marriage, since it is a contract you can leave. But he argues the core truth is the same: when frequencies don't match, no amount of skill or salary fixes it, and the mismatch shows up every Monday morning. His closing advice is not to hire from profiles but to discover frequencies, the idea behind his book Organizational Frequency.

Key points

  • Traditional matchmaking checked compatibility in depth, over time, through people who knew both sides.
  • Companies hire from one resume and one rehearsed interview, with both sides performing.
  • A marriage-sized commitment made with a matrimonial-ad-sized process.
  • A job is a contract, not a vow, but frequency mismatch still can't be fixed by skill or salary.
  • Don't hire from profiles; discover frequencies.
